FAQ

Is this medical advice? Can it tell me if I'll get sick?

No — and it never will. TickSignal identifies the species in a photo and shares public information. It does not diagnose disease, assess your personal risk, or recommend treatment. If you were bitten, see a clinician. Every results surface on this site carries that notice plus links to the CDC and Michigan DHHS.

Is any of this data real?

Not yet — it's in pilot. The site is live, but every number is still labeled SAMPLE, BASELINE, or DEMO. Real numbers appear only after launch.

What happens to my photo and location?

Photos are EXIF-stripped in your browser before upload; only the county you select is stored; the public map shows county-level pins; no names or contact info are collected. Submissions are reviewed before appearing publicly.

How accurate is the AI?

You'll be able to see for yourself on the dashboard — weekly accuracy, the full confusion matrix, and every prediction next to its expert verdict on the results page, misses included. That's the whole point: watch it learn.

Who confirms the AI's guesses?

Volunteer entomologists and public-health partners review the queue. AI suggestions stay suggestions until a human confirms them. Reviewer recruiting is on the roadmap.

Does it cover mosquitoes too?

Yes — Culex and Aedes species ID from photos, same pipeline. Mosquitoes actually photograph better than ticks (wing-vein detail survives phone cameras).

Does it cost anything?

Submitting photos and viewing all data is free forever. Donations — when the ledger opens after founder approval — fund speed and coverage, never access. See funding.

Why Michigan first?

The founder lives in Hastings, Michigan, and the state is the current front line of Lyme expansion (553 cases in 2022 → 2,167 in 2025, per MDHHS). Start where the problem — and the founder — are.

3
Phase 3 — Real classifier. Assemble public tick/mosquito training set (GBIF, research datasets); baseline model in free Colab; publish the honest baseline number, whatever it is.
4
Phase 4 — Validation lane. Recruit volunteer entomologists + Extension/public-health partners; build the expert review queue.
5
Phase 5 — Michigan pilot. 200+ real submissions via Michigan outdoor/parent groups (admin permission only — no spam). First weekly retrain + published accuracy.
6
Phase 6 — Public launch. Founder approval gate. Real map, real dashboard, donation ledger opens. Success bar for day 30: ≥200 submissions, ≥70% expert confirmation, published baseline. Kill bar: real-photo accuracy <60% after fixes, or zero expert volunteers → re-scope before any fundraising.
